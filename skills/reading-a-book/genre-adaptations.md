# Genre adaptations (judgment, not optional)

Reference for `reading-a-book` Step 3: load the entry for the genre you classified in section A. The four questions always apply; the artifacts adapt.

- **Novel / play / poem.** Replace propositions/arguments (F) with plot, characters, themes; replace critique (G) with whether the story rings true and what experience it created. Don't impose logical analysis where it doesn't belong.
- **History.** Read multiple accounts when the user's purpose warrants. Assume narrative shaping. Critique includes "does this reflect a defensible view of human action and causation".
- **Science / mathematics.** For lay reading: focus on the problem the scientist tried to solve and the experimental basis for the claims. Mathematical sections can be skimmed if the goal is the historical or conceptual contribution rather than technical mastery.
- **Philosophy.** Test claims against common experience — yours or anyone's. Look hard for unstated controlling principles (assumptions that pervade the work but are never argued).
- **Practical book.** "What of it" (H) is the dominant artifact. Agreement implies you'll act differently. State the action.
- **Social science.** Often a mix of science, philosophy, history, and advocacy. Identify the strands. Read more than one source on the question.
